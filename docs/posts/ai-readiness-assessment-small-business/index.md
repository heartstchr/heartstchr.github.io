Most small and mid-size businesses do not have an AI problem. They have a *starting* problem. Every tool claims to be AI-powered, every vendor wants a discovery phase, and it is genuinely hard to tell which use of AI will save real hours and which is expensive noise.

An **AI readiness assessment** cuts through that. It answers one practical question: given how your business actually runs today, where can AI pay off — and what do you need to fix first so it is safe?

## What "AI Readiness" Really Means

It is not a measure of how advanced your technology is. It is a measure of whether a specific AI use case can succeed in *your* business right now. Five areas decide that:

- **Data.** Is the information AI would need clean, accessible, and in one place — or scattered across inboxes, spreadsheets, and tools?
- **Tools and integration.** Can AI read and write to the systems you already use, or would it live in a new place nobody checks?
- **Privacy and governance.** What data is safe to send to a model? What must be redacted? Who reviews AI output before a customer sees it?
- **Skills and process.** Who owns the workflow once AI is part of it? What happens when the model is wrong?
- **Economics.** Does the time or revenue saved justify the build and the ongoing model cost?

A use case that passes all five is usually a good bet. One that fails one or two is where projects quietly stall.

## Score Opportunities by Business Impact

Do not start with the most impressive AI use case. Start with the one that is easiest to prove. For most businesses the highest-return early wins are unglamorous:

- Quoting and proposal drafts
- Follow-up and reminder sequences
- Data entry and re-keying between tools
- Support triage and first-draft replies
- Weekly reporting and summaries
- Turning existing content into other formats

Each of these is measurable in hours saved, which makes it easy to justify — or kill — before you invest more.

## Why Pilots and Subscriptions Stall

Two failure patterns show up again and again.

The first is the **dangling pilot**: a trial that never connects to how the business runs, so nobody keeps using it. The second is **tool sprawl**: several overlapping AI subscriptions, none of which talk to each other, quietly adding cost and confusion.

Both are fixable, and both argue for the same approach: assess first, pick one or two wins, and only then scale. If you already pay for AI tools, a good readiness assessment keeps the ones earning their place and replaces or connects the rest.

## Governance Is Not Optional

You can adopt AI quickly and still be responsible. The essentials are simple:

- Know which data may reach a model, and redact personal data where required.
- Define what AI is allowed to decide, and what always needs a human.
- Log and review outputs where mistakes carry real risk.
- Stay aligned with GDPR and emerging rules such as the EU AI Act, which affect how AI can be used and what must be disclosed.

Getting this right early is far cheaper than retrofitting it after an incident or a client security review.

## How to Run Your Own First Pass

You do not need a consultant to get a rough read. Work through a structured checklist: deployment, resilience, scaling, engineering velocity, data, and security — paying special attention to data quality, safe model usage, and governance. ## The Takeaway

AI readiness is not about chasing the newest model. It is about matching a specific, measurable use case to your data, tools, governance, and economics — then proving it in weeks, not quarters. That is how AI goes from an interesting demo to something that genuinely pays for itself.
