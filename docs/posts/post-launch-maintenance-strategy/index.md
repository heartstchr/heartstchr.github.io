Most founders think of their software build as a "Project"—something with a start date, an end date, and a final invoice. They celebrate the "Big Launch," thinking the hard part is over.

In reality, Day One is just the birth of your product. The real work—the iteration, the scaling, and the battle-testing—starts the moment your first real user logs in.

## The Maintenance Gap

The "Maintenance Gap" is where most great ideas go to die. It’s that period 3-6 months after launch when:
*   **Edge Case Bugs Appear:** Users always find ways to break your app that you never anticipated.
*   **Third-Party Updates:** Your payment processor (Stripe) or your email provider (SendGrid) updates their API, and your app suddenly stops working.
*   **Feature Fatigue:** You realize that Feature A is ignored by everyone, but Feature B is a massive hit and needs to be expanded immediately.

If you hired a "contractor" who delivered the code and disappeared, you are now stranded. You have a broken app and no one to fix it.
